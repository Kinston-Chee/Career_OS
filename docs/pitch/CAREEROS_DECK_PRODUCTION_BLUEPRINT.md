# CareerOS Asynchronous Pitch Deck Production Blueprint

Date: 23 July 2026  
Status: production blueprint, revised once after CareerOS deck QA  
Deliverable governed: self-reading PPTX for organisers, judges, Talentbank leadership, and potential investors  
Not governed: live presentation script or demo-video storyboard

## 1. Deck strategy

### Objective

Enable an independent reader to understand and evaluate CareerOS as an evidence-memory and action layer between education and employment: the human problems it addresses, the mechanisms already demonstrated, the breadth and connection of its three workspaces, the limits of the current prototype, and its conditional strategic value to Talentbank.

The deck must leave the reader able to state:

1. Career potential is lost when useful evidence and feedback arrive too late or disappear between systems.
2. CareerOS preserves signals as evidence and turns them into decisions and earlier actions.
3. The student, employer, and university workspaces solve different expressions of the same structural problem.
4. CareerOS is not merely a job portal, chatbot, or dashboard because its core value lies in linked evidence-to-action workflows and accumulated context.
5. The repository demonstrates a polished multi-workspace prototype with local interactions, mock data, scripted intelligence, optional model calls, and a partial backend—not adoption, validated accuracy, or a live cross-organisation network.
6. CareerOS could deepen Talentbank's relevance across the student-employer-university lifecycle if data rights, trust, integrations, buyer demand, and operational ownership are validated.

### Governing narrative

**Career potential is lost when evidence and action arrive too late. CareerOS remembers the signal, turns it into evidence, enables an earlier decision, and preserves what the next stakeholder needs.**

The causal spine is:

`Signal -> Evidence -> Decision -> Action -> Outcome -> Learning`

The emotional version is:

`prevent evidence loss -> prevent option loss -> prevent relationship loss -> prevent time loss -> prevent institutional knowledge loss`

### Reader journey

1. **Recognise one shared failure:** a Data Science student, an employer, and a university experience different consequences of fragmented evidence.
2. **Understand the operating model:** CareerOS is an evidence-and-action system, not three unrelated dashboards.
3. **See student depth:** evidence becomes direction, preparation, action, and reasons to return.
4. **See employer depth:** candidate proof and relationship history become more defensible, reusable talent decisions.
5. **See university depth:** risk and market signals become interventions, partnerships, reusable evidence, and governed decisions.
6. **See the connection:** each workspace can contribute to and benefit from a shared learning loop, while the current implementation remains mostly local and simulated.
7. **Assess credibility:** demonstrated, simulated, and future capabilities are explicitly separated.
8. **Assess strategic fit:** Talentbank's potential lifecycle expansion is framed as a hypothesis to validate, not an accomplished advantage.
9. **Close on a bounded proposition:** CareerOS protects career potential from being lost between moments; the next step is to prove the loop in a consented, measurable pilot.

### Why the deck contains 24 slides

The count follows 24 distinct reader questions. Combining them further would make screenshots too small or reduce major product areas to names; expanding further would repeat mechanisms. The count is therefore a readability and coverage result, not a target.

### Section structure

| Section | Slides | Reader job |
|---|---:|---|
| I. The shared failure | 1-4 | Understand the problem, thesis, model, and truthful product scope |
| II. Student: evidence becomes agency | 5-9 | Understand the Data Science student's complete evidence-to-action journey |
| III. Employer: relationships become reusable talent intelligence | 10-13 | Understand discovery, validation, pipeline, engagement, and learning |
| IV. University: signals become timely institutional action | 14-19 | Understand readiness, curriculum, alumni, collaboration, accreditation, and AI Office |
| V. Connected system, credibility, and strategic value | 20-24 | Understand the loop, truth boundaries, Talentbank fit, path to proof, and closing thesis |

### Feature-depth decisions

| Treatment | Product areas | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Hero depth | Career Memory; Career Intelligence; evidence-aware opportunities/actions; Talent Discovery; Campus Pipeline; Student Readiness; Curriculum-Market Alignment; Accreditation Hub; University AI Office | Central mechanisms, differentiated workflows, or strongest decision proof |
| Supporting depth | Applications; interview practice/AI Companion; engagements; shortlisting/candidate pipeline; Alumni Signals; Collaboration Marketplace | Necessary to show behaviour change and breadth, but subordinate to the core spine |
| Grouped coverage | Communities and mentorships; employer analytics and briefing; event impact and student spotlights; salary/learning recommendations | Important retention or learning mechanisms that share a slide job |
| Mention only | generic home KPIs, settings/help, broad filters, marketplace browsing, decorative salary cards | Adds breadth but no distinct causal proof |
| Exclude | placeholder routes, legacy aliases, old marketplace variants, unsupported predictive/ROI copy | Weak, stale, broken, or misleading evidence |

## 2. Proposed slide architecture and exact content hierarchy

Each slide must work without narration. “Status” below governs visible capability labels.

### Slide 1 — CareerOS: act before potential is lost

**Purpose:** Establish the product thesis and the three-sided scope in one glance.

**Content hierarchy**

1. Eyebrow: `CareerOS — asynchronous product submission`
2. Headline: `Career potential is lost when evidence and action arrive too late.`
3. Subhead: `CareerOS connects student evidence, employer demand, and university action so gaps can be addressed earlier.`
4. Visual: one clean three-sided evidence-loop composition, not three product screenshots
5. Trust line: `Multi-workspace prototype • Demonstrated interactions • Simulated intelligence and data`
6. Footer: date and `Talentbank AI Hackathon`

**Status:** mixed; label the prototype plainly.

### Slide 2 — Three people experience the same failure differently

**Purpose:** Make the student, employer, and university pain concrete before introducing features.

**Content hierarchy**

1. Headline: `The same evidence gap appears as uncertainty, recruiting waste, and late institutional action.`
2. Student moment: `Chris, a Year 3 Data Science student, has projects and leadership experience but weak proof of what they demonstrate and what to do next.`
3. Employer moment: `A recruiter needs talent urgently but prior challenge participants and screening context have gone cold.`
4. University moment: `A programme team sees an MLOps or readiness gap while the current cohort cannot wait for a full curriculum cycle.`
5. Bottom conclusion: `Different symptoms. One structural failure: useful signals are fragmented, weakly evidenced, and remembered too late.`
6. Small evidence note: `Personas and figures shown in-product are demo data.`

**Status:** pains are product hypotheses represented in the prototype; do not claim prevalence.

### Slide 3 — Existing tools optimise transactions, not continuity

**Purpose:** Explain why a job portal, ATS, LMS, annual report, or dashboard alone cannot solve the shared problem.

**Content hierarchy**

1. Headline: `The handoffs fail between the systems.`
2. Current failure chain:
   - student activity becomes a resume claim;
   - employer engagement becomes an attendance list;
   - hiring feedback remains in an ATS or inbox;
   - university outcomes arrive in periodic reports;
   - the next decision starts without the earlier context.
3. Consequence strip: `Evidence loss -> option loss -> relationship loss -> time loss`
4. Design implication: `CareerOS must preserve the context between transactions—not replace every system.`

**Status:** strategic framing, not market-size evidence.

### Slide 4 — CareerOS is an evidence-to-action operating model

**Purpose:** Give the reader the conceptual map needed to interpret all later feature slides.

**Content hierarchy**

1. Headline: `CareerOS turns fragmented signals into a shared sequence of decisions.`
2. Primary diagram: `Signal -> Evidence -> Decision -> Action -> Outcome -> Learning`
3. Under each stage:
   - Signal: project, application, challenge, demand, alumni outcome
   - Evidence: source, recency, context, observed behaviour
   - Decision: next skill, shortlist, intervention, programme priority
   - Action: practise, apply, re-engage, assign, collaborate
   - Outcome: completion, progression, hiring, intervention result
   - Learning: improve guidance and future decisions
4. Three workspace anchors under the sequence
5. Truth boundary: `The prototype demonstrates each workspace and selected handoffs; a live shared intelligence network remains future-facing.`

**Status:** demonstrated model; cross-organisation learning is future.

### Slide 5 — The student starts with evidence, not another profile

**Purpose:** Show why Career Memory exists and how it changes student behaviour.

**Content hierarchy**

1. Headline: `Career Memory turns “I did this” into “Here is what it proves—and what is missing.”`
2. Persona anchor: `Chris Lee • Year 3 • Bachelor of Data Science • Taylor's University`
3. Large annotated screenshot: evidence-oriented timeline or opened memory entry
4. Mechanism callouts:
   - preserve projects, internships, leadership, events, and achievements;
   - connect each experience to skills and supporting evidence;
   - expose missing proof rather than hiding uncertainty.
5. Behaviour change: `Students maintain reusable evidence continuously instead of reconstructing a resume only when applying.`
6. Designed impact: `Stronger career proof and a foundation for guidance, matching, and reflection.`
7. Visible status: `Demonstrated UI • extraction and verification partial/simulated`

### Slide 9 — Return value continues after an application

### Slide 10 — Employers need evidence and history, not another candidate database

**Purpose:** Introduce the employer problem and its decision model before showing screens.

**Content hierarchy**

1. Headline: `Early-talent decisions are weakest when every search starts from CV claims and zero context.`

3. CareerOS employer model: `Need -> evidence-backed discovery -> targeted validation -> decision history -> reusable pipeline`
4. Designed behaviour change: `Ask what supports fit and what must be validated, not merely whether a CV looks right.`
5. Status note: `Current candidates, scores, and relationship histories are demo data.`

**Purpose:** Convert the broad vision into a credible validation path without presenting a committed plan.

**Content hierarchy**

   - one faculty and controlled cohort;

   - evidence completion;
   - intervention uptake/completion;
   - employer participation;
   - time from signal to assigned action;
   - staff time to prepare an evidence summary.
5. Decision gates: trust/consent, buyer ownership, data availability, workflow adoption, measurable operational value.
6. Status: `Recommended validation path • not an announced pilot or partnership`

## 3. Screenshot manifest

### Capture standards

- Capture the current Vite product, not old `pitch-deck/assets/screenshots`.
- Use a 1440 × 900 browser viewport unless the component needs a taller master; export PNG at native resolution.
- Enter through the correct role on `/` or `/auth`; never bypass the role guard in the captured state.
- Hide browser chrome, cursor, transient toast, loading state, developer tools, and personal/secret information.
- Preserve uncropped masters under a production scratch directory; place only approved crops in final deck assets.
- Use one large crop per slide where possible. Never place more than two product screenshots on one slide.
- Treat all visible scores, percentages, rankings, salary figures, demand statistics, and outcome metrics as demo data.

## 4. Visual design system

### Format and composition

- Canvas: 16:9 widescreen, 13.333 × 7.5 inches.
- Safe margins: 0.55 inches left/right; 0.42 inches top; 0.38 inches bottom.
- Grid: 12 columns with 0.16-inch gutters; align titles, body, screenshots, labels, and footers to grid lines.
- Composition: one dominant visual or argument per slide. Use flat canvas structure, not collections of decorative UI cards.
- Section rhythm: narrative slides -> product proof slides -> synthesis slide. Use section eyebrow colour, not divider slides that add no content.

### Palette

| Token | Use | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | primary background | `#F8FAFF` |
| Surface | screenshot frame or evidence panel | `#FFFFFF` |
| Ink | primary text | `#11194A` |
| Body | secondary text | `#52627F` |
| Blue | primary action/evidence | `#2563EB` |
| Indigo | system connection | `#4F46E5` |
| Lavender | secondary emphasis only | `#EDE9FE` / `#7C3AED` |
| Teal | demonstrated status | `#0F766E` / `#CCFBF1` |
| Amber | simulated status | `#B45309` / `#FEF3C7` |
| Slate | future status | `#475569` / `#E2E8F0` |
| Red | QA warning only, never decorative | `#B91C1C` |

Do not place body copy on gradients. Use lavender as an accent, never as low-contrast paragraph text.

### Typography

- Family: Aptos or another installed modern sans serif used consistently.
- Deck title: 50-58 pt, semibold/bold.
- Slide title: 35-40 pt, semibold; maximum two lines, prefer one.
- Section eyebrow: 13-15 pt, bold, uppercase with modest tracking.
- Subhead/callout: 24-28 pt.
- Body: 17-20 pt; never below 16 pt.
- Caption/source/status: 11-13 pt only when non-essential; capability labels at least 12 pt.
- Use sentence case. Avoid title case on long statements.

### Screenshot treatment

- Frame: 1 px cool-grey border, 8-12 px equivalent corner radius, restrained shadow.
- Scale: primary screenshot occupies 48-66% of usable slide area.
- Crop: remove browser chrome and irrelevant navigation while preserving workspace identity.
- Never distort. Use aspect-ratio crop, not scaling to fit.
- Annotation: numbered 20-24 px circles, 1.5 px leader lines, short 3-7 word labels, maximum four per screenshot.
- One zoom inset maximum, used only when a critical feature cannot be read in the primary crop.
- Status label must sit outside the screenshot so it cannot be mistaken for product UI.

### Repeated slide anatomy

1. Section eyebrow
2. Conclusion-led title
3. One-sentence interpretive subhead when necessary
4. Primary proof visual
5. 2-4 mechanism or consequence points
6. Capability-status label
7. Small source/prototype note

### Standalone-readability rules

- Define Career Memory, Career Intelligence, and University AI Office on first use.
- Never rely on animation, speaker notes, or the video to reveal causal steps.
- Explain why each screenshot matters in visible copy.
- Every feature slide must contain `pain/decision`, `mechanism`, `behaviour change`, and `status`, even if compressed into labels.
- Use solid versus dashed connectors consistently for demonstrated versus future handoffs.

## 6. Talentbank strategic framing

### Positioning

CareerOS could help Talentbank expand from episodic job listings, applications, and events into longer-lived student evidence, employer relationship history, and university employability workflows.

### Value paths

1. **Student lifecycle:** engagement before, between, and after applications.
2. **Employer workflow:** evidence-led discovery, warm-pipeline recovery, engagement design, and talent intelligence.
3. **University product:** readiness, alignment, partnerships, outcomes, accreditation, and executive decision support.
4. **Compounding context:** permissioned evidence and relationship history could improve future decisions as usage grows.
5. **Commercial possibilities:** university workflow software, employer intelligence, engagement programmes, and outcome/evidence services are hypotheses—not committed models.

### Truth guardrail

Do not imply that Talentbank currently supplies proprietary datasets, consented cross-side identity, active partner access, institutional customers, or the legal right to combine records. State that Talentbank's position between the three stakeholder groups could make the model strategically relevant, subject to validation.

## 7. Closing structure

The final five slides deliberately close in this order:

1. **Connection:** show why the product is more than separate dashboards.
2. **Truth:** state precisely what exists and what does not.
3. **Strategic value:** explain why Talentbank should care.
4. **Proof path:** show a bounded way to validate the thesis.
5. **Thesis close:** return to the human problem and product definition.

Do not end on a feature list, roadmap theatre, generic “thank you,” or unsupported market claim.
